\textbf{Simulators, microbenchmarks, and effective capacity.}  Kernel-level inference simulators predict serving performance by interpolating profiled kernel grids: Vidur~\cite{vidur} fits learned estimators over per-operator profiling runs, and KernelSight-LM~\cite{yao2026kernelsight} patches a roofline with a learned efficiency term, reaching 12.1\% cross-generation error against 22\% for the plain roofline.  Both repair the deficit this paper names --- a roofline without a capacity term mispredicts cache-resident kernels --- but repair it with fitted degrees of freedom that must be re-profiled per architecture, where we repair it with five measured constants ($C_\text{eff}$, $\mathrm{BW}_{L2}$, $\mathrm{BW}_\text{HBM}$, $r_\text{dequant}$, $t_0$) and a gate: the same form transfers to an unseen B200 at 16.6\%/3.9\% MAPE (below/above gate) with no per-kernel terms (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}).  The Blackwell microbenchmark line~\cite{dissecting2025blackwell,blackwell2025microbench} characterizes the same silicon --- L2 latency and bandwidth, the four-partition topology, TMEM --- and independently corroborates our B200 constants, but characterization is where it stops: none of these studies measure an effective residency capacity, the one number that turns their tables into a predictor.  The same group's microbenchmark-parameterized analytical models~\cite{jarmusch2026analytical} (1.3\% MAE on 21 kernels) are our nearest methodological neighbor, sharing the measure-then-predict mechanism while lacking both the capacity term and the precision decision.  That the capacity term is the portable idea is corroborated off-GPU: CPU-cluster serving that pins weights LLC-resident reports 2.04--11.51$\times$ TPOT gains~\cite{zhang2026cacheresident} --- the residency-changes-dispatch calculus arriving independently on another substrate, and, like our four-GPU effective-vs-nominal fractions (Hopper ${\approx}0.80$, dual-die Blackwell $0.71$--$0.72$), evidence that nominal LLC capacity is the wrong number to reason with.  Arithmetic-intensity-aware precision selection~\cite{aiaq2025} is the capacity-blind limit of our dispatch rule: correct above the gate, wrong below it.

\textbf{On-chip state for linear-attention decode.}  The design-tool result (Section~\ref{sec:designtool}) sits in a fast-moving neighborhood.  An FPGA dataflow accelerator~\cite{gupta2026persistentstate} targets exactly this bottleneck on the premise that GPU L2 cannot persist state across kernel launches --- the premise our counters refute below $C_\text{eff}$ (86\% warm L2 hit rate across graph-replayed decode steps).  SpecLA~\cite{wang2026specla} keeps the state \emph{shared-memory}-resident across the candidate tokens of a single speculative launch (1.70$\times$ on GDN-1.3B/H100) --- the SMEM-tier cousin of our claim, with no capacity model, no batch window, and no cross-launch persistence.  Megakernel systems~\cite{automegakernel2026} fuse whole-model decode into persistent kernels; they occupy the fusion axis of our measured decomposition (fusion ${\sim}1.25\times$ everywhere, residency ${\sim}1.9\times$ inside the window) without placing state under a capacity model or predicting where the locality win dies.  ReplaySSM~\cite{dao2026replayssm} halves state traffic algorithmically, cache-blind, and composes with residency as Section~\ref{sec:designtool} details.  The only other L2-residency inference-kernel work we found stages KV into L2 with \texttt{cp.async.bulk.prefetch.L2}~\cite{kvprefetch2025} --- an operand our measurements show is not L2-limited, where recurrent state is.  The operator family itself is moving beneath all of this --- Qwen's FlashQLA kernels~\cite{flashqla2026} serve chunked prefill only, and Gated DeltaNet-2~\cite{nvidia2026gdn2} changes the update rule but not the state shape --- so the decode-side residency window, whose location the constants predicted on two architectures before the kernel ran (40${\to}$92~MB, tracking measured $C_\text{eff}$), applies to the family's successors as stated.
