When quantization stops paying

A capacity-gated roofline model for precision dispatch on GPUs.
Robert Zhang · Purdue — with FlagGems / BAAI (Dr. Xiao)

why this problem

In real serving, plain GEMM is the whole ballgame

setup

Weight-only quantization promises decode speed by moving fewer bytes

the contradiction

Two careful measurements, same hardware class, opposite answers

MARLIN · batch ≤ 32 ~4×

near-ideal weight-only speedup on large dense GEMMs

this work · MLA-scale GEMMs, batch 1 0.70×

quantized kernel loses to cuBLAS bf16 — no benefit at any size below ~36 MB

Both replicate. Neither is wrong. The variable neither report controls: whether the weights fit in the L2 cache.

the missing term

Roofline can’t referee this — it has no notion of capacity

the claim

One predicate separates the two worlds

W < C_eff · near field

weights already cache-served
→ speedup ≈ 1.0
→ < 1.0 if the kernel dequantizes in-core

W ≫ C_eff · far field

weights stream from DRAM
→ speedup → min(byte ratio, ceiling)
→ MARLIN’s ~4× lives here

W = weight working set · C_eff = measured effective cache capacity — on H100: 36 MB, not the 50 MB on the datasheet.

result · figure 1

Measured: the sign flips exactly at 36 MB

77-cell sweep, clock-locked, CUDA-graph timed, tuned baseline. At decode token counts (T ≤ 32): W8A8 0.7× → 1.2–1.46× across the gate; W4A16 flips at the same place but caps at parity — its in-core dequant ceiling (0.50 TB/s) eats the far field. At T ≥ 64 nothing wins: compute-bound (the known batch-64 collapse).

methodology

The measurements that produce these claims lie easily

boundary condition · 1

Under serving load, the near field dies as a step

Concurrent GEMM streams sharing L2 (real kv_proj shape). The moment Σ hot working sets passes C_eff, the L2 tier is gone — a step, not a slide — and adding more pressure changes nothing. Two consequences: every isolated-microbenchmark crossover (ours included) is optimistic in production; and the smaller quantized operand survives contention that kills the bf16 one. Same principle inside one kernel: residency is set by total footprint (W+act+out), not the weight operand — W=16MB holds L2 through T=128, W=32MB breaks at T=24 (results_footprint_gate.json).

boundary condition · 2

A roofline is necessary — five kernel mechanisms say it isn’t sufficient

A · tile starvation

Marlin’s persistent 132-CTA grid idles at N ≤ 512: fixed 16–29 µs floor. CUTLASS immune.

B · wave-quantization bands

one shape swings 0.75×–2.71× vs M across band edges. Engine-level (prefill, served): adjacent chunk caps 896→1024 step +24%, reproduced — but no cap beats uncapped.

C · dispatch splits

blockwise fp8: CUTLASS path +15–21%; M < 4 falls back to Triton and loses to bf16 at decode.

D · occupancy floors

decode kernels sit at an occupancy floor — the register-pressure hypothesis measured and refuted.

E · re-dequant staircase

Marlin re-dequantizes per M-tile; CUTLASS rides tokens 1→64 free. Visible in Figure 1’s W4A16 collapse.

therefore

Dispatch = CARM + per-kernel predicates. Each mechanism independently flips win ↔ lose at fixed shape.

portability

New silicon: measure three numbers, don’t re-tune

Model fit on H100; A100 constants now measured by the harness (Ceff 31.2 MB = 0.78× nominal — H100’s ratio). fp16 zero-shot on a graph-timed same-GPU target: 12.4% below / 13.2% above the gate — three cheap measured terms: harness constants + a 2-pt baseline-kernel calibration (sm_80 dispatches wmma/gemvx at M=1; the 44% residual was this, not the model) + one footprint-transition band fitted on H100 and transferred normalized. The eager “59 µs kernel fixed cost” is 3.5 µs under graphs — a timing artifact. Kernel terms still don’t scale (naive 61% vs two-point 31%); harness rdequant 0.394 vs sweep-fitted 0.406 TB/s. Runs on any torch-CUDA backend — the FlagOS case.

so how do you ship it?

Storage policy decides whether dispatch is even affordable

A · hold both formats −100 / −33%

second copy is paid in KV budget → concurrency. 27B model: infeasible on 80 GB; −33% throughput on H200.

B · repack on switch ≥ 20 s

switch period for <1% overhead (measured repack, 27B). Engine-mode granularity only — never per-layer or per-phase.

C · JIT dequant via DRAM 2.25×

more traffic than never quantizing. Closed. (In-kernel JIT is the r_dequant ceiling.)

The inversion: store weights quantized-primary — the dispatcher’s question is not “when do we quantize?” but “when do we pay the dequant ceiling vs take the quantized-compute path?” Exactly the world MXFP4-native checkpoints already put us in.

the obvious question

“What about quantizing the KV cache?”

as a speed lever · measured ≤ 0.2%

end-to-end ceiling. KV reads are not L2-limited at any working-set size (rotation-invariant), full attention is only 2.67% of Qwen3.6-27B runtime, and the fp8 decode kernel is bound by its own bandwidth ceiling (1.6 vs bf16’s 3.0 TB/s — it loses 0.69–0.72× at small batch).

as a memory lever · modeled 2× seqs

fp8 KV halves the 2.2 GB/seq budget → max concurrency 9 → 18 on H100-80GB, 36 → 73 on H200. Decode throughput scales with concurrency — this is where KV compression actually pays.

Verdict: outside the gate, inside the budget. KV precision never enters the capacity gate (it isn’t a GEMM operand) — it enters the dispatch problem through the memory budget, where it’s the strongest lever on the table. Quantize KV for concurrency, not for kernel speed.

why this matters more every year

Caches grow — the “quantization doesn’t pay” regime grows with them

1 · Quantization’s payoff is capacity-gated; the gate sits at measured, not nominal, cache size.
2 · Three microbenchmarked numbers predict the win/loss flip — on unseen hardware, at dispatch time.
3 · Ship it quantized-primary: the real decision is dequant ceiling vs quantized compute, per shape.

closing the loop

Does any of this survive a real serving engine? Yes.

decode · 64 seqs · vLLM 1.45×

fp8 W8A8 vs bf16, real Qwen3.6-27B: 2931 vs 2026 gen tok/s. The microbenchmark projection (1.2–1.6×), now a measurement.

prefill · 27k-token prompts 1.20×

15096 vs 12563 tok/s — the compute-bound regime, smaller win, exactly where the model says the benefit thins.

accuracy · wikitext-2 +0.032

ΔPPL (7.528 → 7.560, 49k tokens scored). Per-layer rel-err sits at the gaussian quantization floor, even on the kurtosis-120 outlier layer.

Honest negative alongside: engine-level batch-cap shaping (wave-band demo) only hurt decode throughput (2931 → 2718/2818 tok/s) — kernel-level band effects don’t surface in a decode-dominated engine A/B. Mechanism B stays a prefill batch-shaping concern.
